# Cover Letter Structure & Guidelines

How to write the cover letter: structure, tone, constraints, and examples.

## Structure (4 Paragraphs, 250-300 words total)

### 1. Hook (2-3 sentences) — "Why Them + Why You"

Identify the role and immediately connect expertise to their company's mission or challenges. Reference their engineering blog, recent news, or product.

> "When I saw that [Company] is scaling its payment processing infrastructure to handle global markets, I knew I had to apply for the Staff Engineer role. Having spent the last seven years architecting distributed, high-availability microservices in the fintech space, I specialize in exactly the type of bottleneck resolution your team is currently tackling."

### 2. Value (3-4 sentences) — Best Relevant Accomplishment

Go deeper on ONE accomplishment that directly addresses their biggest need. Don't repeat a resume bullet verbatim. Explain the context, approach, and why it worked.

### 3. Connection (2-3 sentences) — How You Fit Their Challenges

Show understanding of their specific problems. Connect working style or philosophy to what they need right now.

### 4. Close (1-2 sentences) — Call to Action

Brief, confident. Express enthusiasm for discussing specifics. Never beg for an interview.

## Tone Guidelines

- Conversational and confident, not formal/stiff or casual/sloppy
- Like a senior engineer writing to an engineering leader they respect
- Show personality without being unprofessional
- Avoid clichés, filler, and corporate-speak
- Vary sentence length: mix short punchy with longer explanatory

## Greeting

- Use "Dear [Department] Hiring Manager," if name unknown
- Research for the actual hiring manager's name when possible
- Never use "To Whom It May Concern"

## What Cover Letter is NOT

- NOT a prose version of the resume
- NOT about what the job does for you
- NOT a place to explain gaps or apologize
- NOT longer than half a page

## Relationship to Resume

| Resume | Cover Letter |
|--------|-------------|
| Shows WHAT you did | Explains WHY and HOW your approach aligns |
| Bullet-point accomplishments | Narrative with context and personality |
| Keyword-optimized for ATS | Voice-optimized for humans |
| Comprehensive breadth | Deep on ONE most relevant story |
